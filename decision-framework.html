04 — Investor-Side Open Questions

The hard questions we haven't answered

Q1: How do multiple micro-investors make decisions on a single asset?
Options
A. Platform decides (centralized PM) — We appoint a Fal-licensed property manager. Investors receive reports but don't vote on operational decisions. Simplest. Most practical for Saudi market where fractional governance has no legal precedent.

B. Investor vote (DAO-like) — Major decisions (sell property, change PM, capital expenditure) go to investor vote. Democratic but slow. Legal complexity in Saudi law.

C. Hybrid — Platform handles operations. Major exit/sale decisions require majority vote. Balances efficiency with investor control.

Recommendation: Option A for launch. Keep it simple. Add voting for exit decisions only (Option C) once you have traction. HissaTech and Ghanem both use centralized management — investors haven't demanded governance rights.

Q2: Should we run the asset post-tokenization?
Options
A. Third-party PM via Fal-licensed company — Cost: 2% of rent passed to investors. We stay asset-light. Focus on platform. This is the current plan.

B. In-house PM — Higher margin (keep the 2%) but requires hiring, licensing, and operational complexity. Distraction from core platform.

C. Developer retains PM — Cheapest for us. But: conflict of interest (developer already wanted to exit). Trust issue.

Recommendation: Option A. Third-party PM, pass cost through. You're building a platform, not a property management company. In-house PM is a Phase 3+ consideration once you manage 500+ units.

Q3: How do investors exit? (The hardest question)
The problem
Pre-REGA tokenization, there is no secondary market. Investors who want out have no clear path. This is the single biggest investor objection and the weakest point in the entire business case.
Possible solutions
1. Fixed-term hold with scheduled exit — 3-year hold. At year 3, property is either sold (investors get proceeds) or re-offered. Clear, predictable. HissaTech uses 3-year contracts.

2. Annual property sale vote — Each year, investors vote: hold or sell. If majority says sell, property goes to market. Gives investors a voice without promising instant liquidity.

3. Platform matching (bulletin board) — Investor A wants to sell their fraction, Investor B wants to buy. Platform facilitates. Low tech, but requires buyer demand.

4. Platform buyback reserve — Set aside a % of placement fees as a buyback fund. Offer to buy back fractions at a discount (e.g., 90% of NAV). Capital intensive.

5. Wait for REGA tokenization — Once RER tokens are live, secondary market on blockchain. But timeline unknown.

Recommendation: Combine #1 and #3. Launch with 3-year fixed term. Add bulletin-board matching in Year 2. Be transparent: "This is not liquid. If you need instant exit, buy a REIT." Honesty builds trust. The REGA secondary market is the long-term solution but can't be promised.

Mitigations

#2 Trust: Fal-licensed third-party PM. Monthly reports. Ejar-tracked leases. Taqyeem appraisals.
#3 Shariah: Per-asset Shariah opinion (AAOIFI-accredited scholar). Published with each listing.
#4 Regulatory: REGA sandbox or SPV structure. Assets held in separate legal entity, not on our balance sheet.
#5 Operations: Ejar-enforced leases. PM company handles collection. Insurance on vacancy.

06 — Kill / Validate Matrix

What needs to die or survive

Kill signals (stop if)

  • Developers refuse to pay 4–5%
  • Investors consistently say "I'd just buy a REIT" with no counter-argument landing
  • REGA sandbox rejected AND SPV structure is legally unviable
  • Cannot solve liquidity/exit — no path to secondary market or structured exit
  • No co-founder with REGA/fintech experience found by April 2026
  • First deal takes >60 days to fill (proves no investor demand)

Validate signals (proceed if)

  • ✓ 3+ developers say yes to 4–5% — DONE
  • 50+ survey respondents prefer specific-asset ownership over REIT
  • REGA sandbox application submitted by April 30
  • First pilot deal term sheet signed with a developer
  • Legal opinion confirms SPV structure viable without sandbox
  • Co-founder identified with regulatory credibility
  • First deal fills in <21 days (matches HissaTech benchmark)

The next assumption to kill-test: "Do investors care enough about specific-asset ownership to accept higher fees and less liquidity vs REITs?"

07 — Decision Gates

Time-bound go/no-go points

Each gate has a clear question, criteria, and cost. If a gate fails, stop or pivot — don't push through on hope.

1
Gate 1 — March 2026 (NOW)
Can you articulate why investors should choose you over REITs?
Must be true: You have a clear, honest answer to "why not just buy a REIT?" that doesn't rely on hand-waving about "blockchain" or "tokenization."
How to test: Write the answer. Show it to 5 people. If they find it compelling, proceed.
If it fails: The value prop isn't strong enough. Pivot to a different angle or stop.
Cost: SAR 0 + 5 hours. Fully reversible.
2
Gate 2 — April 15, 2026
REGA sandbox application ready? Co-founder identified?
Must be true: Application form completed. Legal entity registered. Regulatory counsel engaged. At least one co-founder candidate with REGA/fintech experience.
How to test: Is the application ready to submit? Yes or no.
If it fails: Submit to next sandbox round. Launch with SPV structure. Delay is acceptable; no sandbox is not fatal.
Cost: SAR 20–40K (legal + entity). Partially reversible.
3
Gate 3 — June 2026
Is there real investor demand signal?
Must be true: Landing page with 500+ signups, or survey with 50+ "would invest" responses, or LOIs from institutional investors (e.g., via Manafa/Forus).
How to test: Run targeted ads (SAR 5–10K budget) to a landing page. Measure conversion. Or leverage partner platforms for distribution test.
If it fails: Investor demand is not there. The REIT comparison wins. Stop or pivot to a B2B-only model (developer exit without fractional investors — e.g., institutional buyer network).
Cost: SAR 10–20K (landing page + ads). Reversible.
4
Gate 4 — September 2026
First pilot deal structured and funded?
Must be true: Developer lists a unit. Legal structure (SPV or sandbox) live. Fractions offered to investors. Deal fills within 30 days.
How to test: Did real investors put real money in? How fast?
If it fails: The model doesn't work in practice. Cut losses. Total exposure at this point: SAR 50–100K + 6 months of part-time work. Painful but survivable.
Cost: SAR 50–100K cumulative. Hard to reverse — this is the commitment gate.

Total cost to reach Gate 3 (real demand signal): SAR 30–60K + part-time effort. That's the price of knowing whether this is real or not. Everything before Gate 4 is reversible.
